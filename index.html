9th GradeSchool ProjectAutodesk Inventor

Mario Automata

An automata is a mechanism that turns one rotating shaft into scripted motion. For this project I designed and built one around a classic Mario moment: Mario jumps up and hits a lucky block, then a coin pops out, spins, and falls back down. A hand crank drives everything.

The project had to run off one main shaft with a crank and produce at least two different motions.

3Spur gears
3Cam shafts
4+Design revisions
Full project deck (PDF)
Build log: design, problems, fixes
  1. Ideas

    I sketched a rail-and-cars concept and a shark made of rising pieces. Both needed near-perfect timing and fitting, so I moved on.

  2. Final idea and CAD

    I chose the Mario jump and coin, sketched it in my notebook, and modeled the box, shafts, and gears in Autodesk Inventor.

  3. Prototype 1

    The coin motion and spur gears ran smoothly. Mario's motion did not. My snail cam was too small, so the follower hit a cam shaft, and Mario moved side to side instead of up and down.

  4. Fix, test, repeat

    I enlarged the cam and added stoppers to hold the follower in place. When they slid off, I made bigger stoppers. Mario still lacked the weight to jump, so I added a small piece of tape that applies the same pressure my finger did.

  5. Final build

    Laser-cut wood box, 3D-printed parts, and a painted grass top. Mario now jumps before the coin moves, and the coin is on time. One flaw remains: the coin does not drop back into the lucky block.

Final design parts
  • 3 spur gears, 3 cam shafts
  • Snail cams and followers
  • Stoppers and a crank
  • Mario, coin, and lucky block
What I learned

Parts that work alone can still fail together. Cam size, follower position, and weight all affect each other, and each fix showed me the next problem.

9th GradeSchool ProjectReverse Engineering

Trident Pen

For my reverse engineering project I designed a working pen shaped like a trident. I traced a trident image in Autodesk Inventor, then modeled the pen body and cap, and 3D printed them. I chose a trident because it looked cool and was not a basic design.

The final print works and writes.

30Sketches
3Prints
8Tries on one cap join
Full project deck (PPTX)
Build log: design, problems, fixes
  1. Concept and sketch

    I imported a trident image into Inventor and traced it as my sketch, using it as a reference and then making my own version of the design.

  2. Pen body and cap

    I used the revolve tool the most, since the handle and prongs are mostly revolved shapes. Joining the cap tip to the middle section was the hardest part and took 8 tries. I mirrored one half to finish, and I added a hidden ring inside the cap so it holds on better.

  3. Test print 1

    The cap worked, but the body was too thin for the pen to fit. I made it thicker and stronger.

  4. Test print 2

    Everything was there, but something blocked it from working. I found the problem and changed it for the final print.

  5. Final print

    I adjusted the grip, thickness, and tip, and went from 1 to 2 layers. It works and writes. I also found that my pen screws on rather than clipping on, which turned out better than my original plan.

Tools and methods
  • Autodesk Inventor
  • Revolve, mirror, and revolve cuts
  • 3D printing
What I learned

Exact measurements and symmetry matter. Modeling one half and mirroring it kept the three prongs lined up, and keeping sketches organized kept the file manageable.
